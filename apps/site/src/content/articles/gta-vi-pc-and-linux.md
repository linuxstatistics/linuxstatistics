GTA VI came out on November 19, 2026 for PS5 and Xbox Series X|S only. [Rockstar's game page](https://www.rockstargames.com/VI) lists those two platforms and says nothing about a PC version or PC requirements. So there is nothing official to run on Linux yet, and any "GTA VI PC requirements" you find today is a guess.

That doesn't stop the guessing. Since launch, the news has mixed Rockstar's own statements, press reports and claims from fan projects, often in the same headline. This article explains how we sort them on our [GTA VI PC check page](/can-i-run/gta-vi/), so you can judge for yourself what a given story is worth.

## Three labels, three levels of trust

Every item on our page carries one of three labels.

**Confirmed** means Rockstar said it, on its own site. Today that covers one thing: the game is out on PS5 and Xbox Series X|S. It does not cover a PC version, because Rockstar hasn't announced one.

**Reported** means a news outlet said it and Rockstar hasn't confirmed it. We only use outlets whose article we opened and read, and we describe the claim in our own words with "press reports that" in front. For example, [XDA reports](https://www.xda-developers.com/unofficial-gta-6-pc-port-making-progress-developers-have-first-in-game-visuals/) that an unofficial effort has booted a leaked console build on PC and shows basic visuals at a very poor frame rate, and notes it has not verified the clips. [Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/GTA-6-PC-port-progressing-at-breakneck-speed.1458028.0.html) reports that modders got the same kind of build through its initial setup, and says the report rests on a modder's post. Both are worth reading with that in mind.

**Unverified** means a project's own claim that no independent source has confirmed. We list these without a link. Some of the unofficial projects say they have runs on Linux. We can't check that, and no independent source has confirmed it.

## What we leave out, and why

You will notice we don't name the unofficial projects, link to their code or chat servers, or explain how to get or run a leaked build. There are three reasons.

First, safety. "GTA VI PC download" pages are a common malware lure, and a leaked build is exactly what those pages claim to offer. We'd rather you didn't go looking, and [checking the hash of any file you download](/guides/verify-download/) is a habit worth having anyway.

Second, rights. Running or sharing a leaked copy may break copyright law and a game's terms, and can get accounts banned. Press also reports that Rockstar is [acting against related communities](https://www.notebookcheck.net/As-GTA-6-PC-port-gets-closer-to-becoming-playable-Rockstar-is-reportedly-taking-action-against-modders-and-Discord-communities.1458181.0.html), and that [the largest effort has gone offline](https://decrypt.co/383860/gta-6-pc-ports-nuked-just-days-before-devs-expected-it-to-be-playable). Reports differ on why, and it is unclear whether any legal notice was sent.

Third, it isn't a PC version. A console build booting on a PC is not a playable game, and so far no playable build is confirmed. For a Linux player, the useful question is whether it will run well on your hardware once Rockstar ships it.

## What we can't tell you

We can't tell you how GTA VI will run on Linux, or whether it will run at all, because there is no PC release and so no Steam or Proton path to test. We can't tell you whether its online mode will work either. Online games often block Linux through anti-cheat, so look the game up on [AreWeAntiCheatYet](https://areweanticheatyet.com/) and [ProtonDB](https://www.protondb.com/) once it ships.

What we can do is give you a fair yardstick. An earlier Rockstar PC game, Red Dead Redemption 2, has official PC requirements and runs on Linux through Proton: ProtonDB rates it Gold from 1,474 reports, as we last checked on November 24. We compare your graphics card, processor and memory with those requirements and with a PS5-class estimate of our own, and we label the estimate as an estimate. Nothing about your hardware leaves your browser: you pick from lists, and the result lives only in the address of the page.

## What you can do today

- **Check your PC** on [the GTA VI page](/can-i-run/gta-vi/). It takes a minute, and the result is a rough placement, not a promise.
- **Get your Linux setup ready.** Most big games that reach Proton need a Vulkan-capable graphics card and a current driver, and ideally 16 GB of memory or more. See [the best Linux for gaming](/best/gaming/) if you're choosing a distro.
- **Treat headlines with a label in mind.** When a story says a PC port is "close", ask who said it. If it's a project, it's unverified. If it's an outlet, it's reported. If it's Rockstar, it's confirmed, and so far it hasn't said anything about PC.

## When this changes

If Rockstar announces a PC version, we replace our estimate with its requirements as soon as we've checked them. We review the page by hand and show a warning when it has gone more than two weeks without a check. The date at the top of the page says when it was last updated.
